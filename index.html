About

My research connects machine learning with experimental chemistry to understand how molecular structure and environment control chemical reactivity. I develop models that learn from incomplete observations, distinguish competing explanations, and guide experiments that improve their predictions. My goal is to turn this understanding into the ability to design catalysts and reaction conditions for new molecules.

At NYU, I am advised by Prof. Kyunghyun Cho. Previously, I was a Visiting Researcher at Meta FAIR (2024–2026), mentored by Dr. Ben Miller and Dr. Ricky Chen. At MIT, I was advised by Prof. Yogesh Surendranath during my PhD and by Prof. Yuriy Roman during my postdoc.

Research Interests

  • Learning chemistry from imperfect evidence. Developing models that connect scientific claims to experimental support, reason under uncertainty, and revise their conclusions as new evidence emerges.
  • Molecular generation across environments. Learning how molecular structures and energies change in solution, crystals, and protein binding pockets, with the goal of extending these insights to transition states and reaction barriers.
  • Discovering reaction mechanisms. Combining simulation, spectroscopy, and experimental kinetics to infer hidden reaction steps, identify unexpected products, and select measurements that distinguish competing pathways.
  • Designing catalysts and electrochemical interfaces. Using mechanistic predictions and experimental feedback to design ligands, catalysts, and reaction environments that control rates and selectivity across substrates.
